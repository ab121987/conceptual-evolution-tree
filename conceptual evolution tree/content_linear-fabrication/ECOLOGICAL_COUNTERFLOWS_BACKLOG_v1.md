## Core distinction

The existing Sankey maps a predominantly linear fabrication system.

Current return loops include:

- End of Use → Use: direct reuse;
- End of Use → Fabrication / Manufacture: component reuse or remanufacture;
- End of Use → Material Processing: recycling/downcycling.

Recycling does **not** normally return material to Virgin Resources. It usually returns material to processing and requires additional energy.

A future speculative layer should ask what relationships would move beyond late-stage loop closure toward open, reciprocal and regenerative material systems.

## Possible speculative input/counterflow families

### Stewardship → Virgin Resources

Possible ideas:

- stewardship;
- custodianship;
- resource-based economies;
- community resource governance;
- distributed records/ledgers for forest stewardship;
- mechanisms through which people interface with and care for resource ecologies.

Reference example supplied by the user:

https://autonomousforest.org/about

Possible questions:

- What obligations accompany the right to take a resource?
- Can resource availability be governed by ecological condition rather than demand alone?
- Can people become custodians or interfaces within resource systems rather than external consumers?
- Can extraction rights be coupled to long-term restoration and care?

### Ecological Condition ↔ Virgin Resources

Possible relationships:

- renewal rate;
- habitat condition;
- soil formation;
- water availability;
- forest succession;
- carbon storage;
- biodiversity thresholds.

Questions:

- Can ecological condition determine the amount, timing and method of extraction?
- What signals would pause or reduce throughput?
- Can resources retain agency within the decision system?

### Extraction ↔ Restoration / Reciprocity

Possible branches:

- restoration;
- habitat creation;
- soil repair;
- seasonal extraction;
- reversible disturbance;
- resource return.

Questions:

- What must be returned for what is removed?
- Can extraction produce greater ecological capacity rather than only mitigation?
- Can disturbance remain open to ecological feedback?

### Material Processing ↔ Mutual Exchange

Possible branches:

- waste heat exchange;
- water recirculation;
- biological catalysis;
- by-product exchange;
- nutrient-safe outputs;
- ambient/low-energy transformation.

Questions:

- Can one process output become a safe input for another organism or material system?
- Can processing conditions respond to ecological capacity?
- Can transformations occur without repeatedly erasing material history?

### Fabrication ↔ Use

Possible counterflows:

- feedback from use;
- maintenance;
- repair;
- adaptation;
- modular replacement;
- remanufacture;
- redistribution.

Do not include material passports.

Questions:

- Can the material remain alterable after fabrication?
- Can use conditions change the form, stiffness, function or lifespan of an artefact?
- Can the artefact communicate emerging needs back to fabrication?

### Use ↔ Redistribution

Possible branches:

- sharing;
- cascading functions;
- changes of ownership;
- component exchange;
- temporary use;
- alternative second and third lives.

Questions:

- Can value be retained through redistribution rather than ownership and replacement?
- Can a material move between functions without being fully reprocessed?

### End of Use → Ecology

This is different from recycling.

Possible relationships:

- safe biodegradation;
- nutrient return;
- soil formation;
- habitat creation;
- mineral return;
- ecological scaffolding.

Questions:

- Under what conditions can a manufactured material become a beneficial ecological input?
- How can materials avoid distorting carbon, nutrient or water cycles?
- Can end-of-use activity rejuvenate an ecology rather than merely reduce damage?

## Visual status

Future ecological counterflows should use a clearly different visual language from measured/estimated Sankey ribbons.

Possible treatment:

- fine green/blue lines;
- soft gradients;
- dotted or open strokes;
- explicit label: `SPECULATIVE ECOLOGICAL COUNTERFLOW`;
- questions and provocations in the fixed information panel.

They must not be confused with sourced or estimated quantitative evidence.

## No comparative case-study lens

Do not add “best” and “worst” material case studies inside the main Sankey.

Specific systems such as prosthetics or buildings can later become separate drill-down pages or linked nodes rather than competing with the overview.
